Piedmont · 500 ft

Read the corridor, west to east

Ten provinces, in the order you would cross them driving from the mountains to the sea. Each one opens on the map with its native plants, soils, invasive species, and planting calendar.

Along the transect

  • Valley and Ridge

    Long parallel folds of the Great Appalachian Valley. Limestone valleys, sandstone ridges, and the sharpest soil changes over the shortest distances anywhere in the corridor.

    West of the crest

  • Blue Ridge

    The crest itself. Ancient metamorphic rock, thin acidic soils, the coldest hardiness zones in the corridor, and the headwaters for nearly every river below.

    Ridge · zones 5b–7a

  • Piedmont

    Rolling clay country between the mountains and the seam. Red soils heavy with iron, gentle relief, and the longest growing season you can get without sand.

    Foot of the mountain

  • The Fall Line

    Where hard rock meets soft sediment and the rivers drop. The reason a dozen cities sit where they do — and the sharpest ecological boundary you will cross.

    The seam

  • Coastal Plain

    Tidewater. Sand and marine clay laid down by an ocean that used to be here, flat drainage, high water tables, and the mildest winters in the corridor.

    Tidewater · zones 7b–9a

  • Gulf Coastal Plain

    The corridor's southwestern arm, running out past Columbus. Deep sands, longleaf pine country, and the longest frost-free window on the map.

    Southwest arm

Northern reach

  • New England Upland

    Glaciated hills and stony till north of the Hudson. Boulders in every field, short seasons, and soils built by ice rather than weathering.

    Glaciated

  • New England Coastal Lowland

    The narrow maritime strip. Ocean-moderated winters, salt exposure, and a growing season that runs later into autumn than anything inland at the same latitude.

    Maritime

West of the divide

  • Great Lakes Basin

    Lake-effect country. Deep winter snow that insulates roots, a late spring, and the lake moderation that makes fruit growing possible this far north.

    Lake-moderated

  • Interior Lowlands & Ohio Valley

    Beyond the crest, draining the other way. Deep fertile soils, broad river bottoms, and the corridor's most productive agricultural ground.

    West-draining
